geographical regions of india

north india

the northern regions including the indo-gangetic plains, himalayas, and northern plateaus. this region saw rise of major empires including mauryan, gupta, mughal, and delhi sultanate. centers: delhi, agra, pataliputra.

region major kingdoms key cities
gangetic plains mauryan, gupta, mughal pataliputra, benares, delhi, agra
punjab mauryan, mughal lahore, amritsar, chandigarh
rajasthan rajput kingdoms, mughal jaipur, jodhpur, udaipur, chittorgarh

south india

the southern peninsula with deccan plateau, western and eastern ghats. dominated by dravidian kingdoms including chola, pandya, vijayanagara, and hoysala empires. centers: madurai, thanjavur, hampi.

region major kingdoms key cities
tamil nadu chola, pandya madurai, thanjavur, kanchipuram
karnataka hoysala, vijayanagara hampi, bangalore, mysore
andhra pradesh satavahana, chalukya, vijayanagara hyderabad, vijayawada, warangal

east india

the eastern regions including bengal, orissa, and assam. centers of culture and learning. major kingdoms: pala, sena, mughal.

  • bengal - center of buddhist learning and commerce
  • nalanda - major university and monastic center
  • orissa - temple architecture and maritime activity
  • assam - ahom kingdom and regional powers

west india

the western coast and gujarat region. important trading centers and maritime hubs.

  • gujarat - sultanate, mughal control, trading posts
  • maharashtra - maratha empire stronghold
  • coastal regions - portuguese, dutch, british trading posts
  • rajput kingdoms - warrior states and principalities

geographical features

mountains

  • himalayas - northern boundary and defensive barrier
  • western ghats - separates coast from interior
  • eastern ghats - eastern mountain ranges
  • vindhyas - central plateau boundary

rivers

  • ganges - sacred river of north india, civilizational artery
  • brahmaputra - major river of east india
  • godavari - deccan region river
  • krishna - southern dravidian river
  • indus - northwestern river (now in pakistan)

plateau regions

  • deccan plateau - major geographical feature of south
  • malwa plateau - central india
  • indo-gangetic plains - north india's fertile zone

climate zones

  • himalayan - alpine and temperate
  • indo-gangetic - subtropical monsoon
  • deccan - semi-arid and tropical
  • coastal - tropical maritime

historical significance

the diverse geographical regions of india created distinct cultural, political, and economic zones that developed unique kingdoms, art forms, languages, and traditions. regional variations shaped the development of indian history and continue to define cultural diversity.
