dynasty details

mauryan dynasty (322-185 bce)

founder: chandragupta maurya

capital: pataliputra

region: north and central india

major rulers:

  • chandragupta maurya - founder with military strategy
  • bindusara - expansion and stability
  • ashoka - conversion to buddhism, dharma spread

decline: internal conflicts, external pressures, eventually replaced

gupta dynasty (320-550 ce)

founder: chandragupta i

capital: pataliputra

region: north and central india

major rulers:

  • samudragupta - military expansion
  • chandragupta ii - golden age peak
  • kumaragupta - continued prosperity
  • skandagupta - final major ruler

achievements: mathematics advances, classical literature, temple construction, scientific progress

chola dynasty (850-1270 ce)

founder: vijayalaya chola

capital: thanjavur

region: south india and maritime

major rulers:

  • raja raja chola i - expansion and temple building
  • rajendra chola i - maritime expansion to southeast asia

achievements: maritime power, spice trade, temple architecture, bronze sculpture

tughlak dynasty (1320-1414 ce)

founder: ghazi malik (ghiyasuddin tughlak)

capital: delhi

major rulers:

  • ghiyasuddin tughlak - founder and organizer
  • muhammad tughlak - expansionist, experimental
  • firuz shah tughlak - welfare schemes

legacy: administrative innovations, architectural projects, eventually fragmented

mughal dynasty (1526-1857 ce)

founder: babur

capital: delhi, agra

region: most of india

major rulers:

  • babur - founder through military conquest
  • akbar the great - peak period, religious tolerance
  • jahangir - cultural patronage
  • shah jahan - architectural splendor, taj mahal
  • aurangzeb - final expansion, religious orthodoxy

achievements: indo-islamic architecture, taj mahal, urdu language, administrative system, cultural synthesis

decline: regional fragmentation, british expansion, ultimately overthrown in 1857

maratha dynasty (1674-1818 ce)

founder: shivaji maharaj

region: india (multiple territories)

major rulers:

  • shivaji maharaj - founder and military strategist
  • sambhaji - continued resistance
  • peshwas - administrative heads

significance: resistance to mughal authority, hindu resurgence, administrative decentralization, eventually defeated by british
