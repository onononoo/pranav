mauryan empire (322-185 bce)

founded by chandragupta maurya with help of kautilya. capital at pataliputra. major rulers: chandragupta, bindusara, ashoka. ashoka converted to buddhism and spread dharma through rock edicts. extensive military campaigns brought large territory under control.

key achievements

  • centralized administration and bureaucracy
  • extensive military power
  • ashoka's conversion to buddhism
  • construction of stupas and monasteries
  • development of mauryan art and sculpture
  • extensive rock edicts promoting dharma

gupta empire (320-550 ce)

called the golden age of india. founded by chandragupta i. major rulers: samudragupta, chandragupta ii. period of remarkable cultural and scientific advancement. advances in mathematics including zero and decimal system. classical sanskrit literature including kalidasa's works. aryabhata's astronomical calculations.

key achievements

  • advancement in mathematics - zero and decimal system
  • classical sanskrit literature and drama
  • temple architecture and sculpture
  • aryabhata's astronomical calculations
  • advances in medicine and metallurgy
  • flourishing arts and culture

mughal empire (1526-1857 ce)

founded by babur who defeated ibrahim lodi. indo-islamic dynasty. major rulers: akbar, shah jahan, aurangzeb. reached peak under akbar with religious tolerance and administrative reforms. shah jahan commissioned taj mahal. mughal architecture blended islamic and hindu styles. extensive control over most of india.

key achievements

  • taj mahal and mughal architecture
  • akbar's administrative reforms
  • religious tolerance and cultural synthesis
  • development of urdu language
  • expansion of trade and commerce
  • patronage of arts and literature

chola empire (850-1270 ce)

maritime and military power in south india. founded by vijayalaya chola. major rulers: raja raja chola i, rajendra chola i. controlled spice trade routes and established trading posts in southeast asia. famous for temple construction.

key achievements

  • maritime power and naval fleet
  • spice trade control
  • brihadisvara temple construction
  • bronze sculpture and art
  • administrative efficiency
  • influence across southeast asia
